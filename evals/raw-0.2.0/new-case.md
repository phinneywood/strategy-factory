## 1. Problem and decision

**User proposal:** Start a neighborhood home-maintenance reminder service, assuming people will pay $15/month because they forget maintenance. Source: user's request, 2026-09-21.

**Decision to develop:** Is there a recurring maintenance problem for an identifiable group of neighbors that a paid reminder service can solve?

**Evidence supplied:** The idea and proposed price. No customer examples, interviews, payments or observed maintenance outcomes have been supplied.

**Assumptions:** Forgetting is a material cause of missed maintenance; reminders will prompt action; customers value the result enough to pay $15/month; value recurs enough to retain subscribers.

**Strong alternative explanation:** People may already remember the work but lack time, money, knowledge or a trusted provider. A reminder alone would leave those barriers unresolved. This is a competing hypothesis, not an observed fact.

Decision-maker: user. Desired business outcome, target customer, neighborhood, budget and available time: not yet established.

2026-09-21 — Initial framing: retain the user's $15/month prediction as an untested assumption. No decision to build or launch has been recorded.

## 3. Value and adoption

Proposed mechanism: relevant reminder → customer completes maintenance → fewer missed tasks → value sufficient to subscribe and renew. Every causal link remains untested.

At the proposed price, a customer would pay $180/year (arithmetic: $15 × 12). Revenue is not profit; acquisition, setup, follow-up and support costs are unknown. Avoided repair costs cannot yet be claimed.

Possible adoption friction includes supplying home details, trusting the advice and taking action after a reminder. These are hypotheses. What makes the neighborhood focus useful is also unresolved.

## 4. Validation

Priority hypothesis: A reachable group of neighbors repeatedly misses important maintenance because they forget, and would pay $15/month for help that leads to completion.

First resolve the customer and barrier through concrete recent examples. Interest alone would not validate payment or retention. A paid offer may then be appropriate, but test design, owner, cost, duration, measures, thresholds and stop criteria are **not yet explored**. Set them before running a test; no outreach or pilot has been authorized or conducted here.
